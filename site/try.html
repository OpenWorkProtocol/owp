Scope

Field Lab results are not conformance certification

A local or hosted field lab demonstrates durable workflow. It does not by itself prove OWP conformance, validator independence, or exact-result Software Work Integrity.

Security model →

What happens there

A visitor can submit a GitHub PR or build idea, describe the outcome they want, and provide a non-binding value signal. The operator explicitly ACCEPTs or PASSes. Accepted work can move through numbered Attempts, durable questions, delivery evidence, named validation, customer APPROVE/STEER/REJECT, and a portable handoff export.

No money is collected

The value signal is research input. It is not payment, escrow, debt, priority, or a promise of service.

Why it is on a subdomain

openworkprotocol.org explains the protocol and its claims. try.openworkprotocol.org runs an experiment. Keeping those surfaces separate makes the distinction between product behavior and protocol requirements explicit.

Security boundary

The intake service should remain bound to loopback behind a trusted tunnel/reverse proxy and must not execute submitted untrusted repositories inside the public web process. Customer tracking links and deployment credentials are secrets. The current Field Lab's named validation record is not, by itself, full RC3 exact-base verification.

Field Lab scope.

Try OWP does not claim full OWP 1.0 conformance, independent interoperability, a marketplace, payment processing, or full Software Work Integrity validation.
